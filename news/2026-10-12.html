Protein design October 9, 2026

GSK signs on to use Chai Discovery's AI models to design antibody-type drugs, after its own lab tests found binders for every target it tried

GSK, one of the world's largest drug makers, has agreed to use the protein-design AI models of Chai Discovery, a start-up valued at $3.8 billion. The move came after GSK tested the models in its own laboratories. Endpoints counts GSK as Chai's sixth large drug-company partner in nine months, after Eli Lilly, Pfizer, Novartis, Argenx and Bristol Myers Squibb.

The AI does the design work: Chai's models propose protein sequences that should bind a chosen disease target, and they do so "zero-shot," meaning they were not retrained on each target first. Chai's earlier model, Chai-2, was reported to succeed 16% of the time in fully de novo antibody design. A newer model, Chai-3, is aimed at harder targets. GSK's Christopher Austin said the company "saw designs with strong binding affinities" across a panel of diverse targets, and Fierce Biotech reports GSK found binders to all targets it tested.

Why it matters: GSK has not said how many targets it tested, how strong the binding was, or what the deal is worth, so "binders for every target" cannot yet be judged from outside. A molecule that binds a target is also only the first step: it must still be made, shown safe and tested in people. The signal here is commercial: large companies keep paying to use these models.

Sources: Fierce Biotech · Endpoints News

Discovery October 6, 2026

insitro's AI models for predicting how a drug behaves in the body are now available to select biotech companies through Lilly

insitro, an AI drug company, has made machine-learning models available that predict how a small-molecule drug behaves in the body. Select biotech companies can use them through Lilly TuneLab, an AI platform run by Eli Lilly. Predicting this early matters because, in insitro chief executive Daphne Koller's words, nine in 10 programs that enter the clinic fail.

The AI does the work by learning from Lilly's decades of animal and laboratory measurements on hundreds of thousands of molecules. From a new molecule's structure, the models predict its pharmacokinetics: how much reaches the bloodstream and how long it stays there, one part of what ADMET covers. Today this is usually estimated from test-tube assays and confirmed in animals. TuneLab runs on federated learning infrastructure, and the models will be updated as the data grow.

Why it matters: The announcement gave no accuracy figures, so how well the models predict real animal and human results is not yet public. Access is limited to select biotech companies, and the training data are Lilly's own. If the predictions hold up, they could let teams drop weak molecules before animal studies; that has not yet been shown.

Sources: insitro

Funding October 8, 2026

AI drug company Iambic plans a stock market listing to raise about $135 million, with three cancer drugs yet to show results

Iambic Therapeutics, an AI drug company founded in 2019, has filed to sell 9.4 million shares at $15 to $17 each. At the midpoint that raises about $150 million, or about $135 million after costs. It is one of the few AI-first drug companies to seek a public listing, and the filing sets out where the money would go.

Iambic describes its approach as a "molecular superintelligence platform," built on AI models called Enchant and NeuralPLexer, and plans to spend $20 million of the proceeds on training them further. The larger sums go to three cancer drugs: about $75 million for IAM1363, an oral, brain-penetrant Phase 1 drug aimed at HER2-altered tumors; $15 million to bring IAM217, aimed at ovarian and triple-negative breast cancer, into the clinic; and $15 million to start a Phase 1 study of IAM-C1, aimed at breast cancer.

Why it matters: Fierce Biotech reports no efficacy or safety results for any Iambic drug, so the AI's contribution cannot yet be judged from patient data. The IPO has not priced; the figures above are a proposed range. A Takeda agreement from February is worth up to $1.7 billion, but that is in milestone payments that depend on future progress.

Sources: Fierce Biotech
