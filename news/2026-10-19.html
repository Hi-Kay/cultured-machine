Discovery October 15, 2026

AstraZeneca pays AI company insitro $40 million up front, with up to about $1.46 billion more in milestones, to find drug targets for liver and heart disease

AstraZeneca has signed a deal with insitro, an AI drug company, to find new drug targets for two hard-to-treat conditions: metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), a form of fatty liver disease, and cardiomyopathies, diseases of the heart muscle. insitro receives $40 million up front and is eligible for up to about $1.46 billion in later payments tied to development and sales, plus single-digit royalties.

The AI does the target-finding. insitro will use its Virtual Human platform, which combines large human and cellular datasets with machine-learning tools, to look for the causal drivers of each disease: the biological changes that cause it, rather than ones that merely accompany it. insitro will then take targets that AstraZeneca nominates through to a development candidate, the molecule chosen to enter formal testing. AstraZeneca will handle clinical development and regulatory work.

Why it matters: The $1.5 billion headline is almost entirely milestone payments, which are paid only if programs succeed; the certain money is the $40 million. Neither company has released data showing that the platform has found a target that holds up in patients. Choosing the right target is, as AstraZeneca's Sharon Barr put it, one of the most important decisions in drug research, so the deal is a bet on that step.

Sources: AstraZeneca · Contract Pharma · Endpoints News

Protein design October 14, 2026

Researchers used AI to design, from scratch, new enzymes that cut proteins at chosen sites, and showed they work inside human cells

A University of Washington team, from the Baker laboratory, has designed new protease enzymes that do not exist in nature, as reported in Nature on October 14. A protease is an enzyme that cuts other proteins at specific points. Designing one is hard because the enzyme must hold its target in exactly the right position to break a chemical bond.

AI protein-design tools did the work: they generated the 3D structures and amino-acid sequences for the enzymes, an approach known as de novo design. Of 69 initial designs, 13 cut their intended targets. The team improved the best into a version that speeds up the cutting reaction about 1.8 billion times compared with no enzyme. Six crystal structures matched the computer models to within 1.2 angstroms, and the enzymes cut a reporter protein inside human HeLa cells.

Why it matters: This is a research result, not a product. The best designs are still about 2.5 times slower than natural proteases, and the suggested uses, such as cutting proteins involved in neurodegenerative disease, have not been tested. What it shows is that AI can now produce working enzymes for a reaction with no natural template, not only proteins that bind.

Sources: HotHardware (reports the Nature paper, "De novo design of cysteine proteases") · Preprint on bioRxiv

Discovery October 14, 2026

Deep Origin says its computational screening found several structurally distinct hits for a BioAge Labs target in under three months, without disclosing numbers

Deep Origin and BioAge Labs, a company that picks drug targets from human longevity data, have announced a multi-target collaboration and the first milestone: small-molecule hits against one target, confirmed in BioAge's laboratory. A hit is a molecule that shows some activity against the target, the first step toward a drug.

The computer does the screening. Deep Origin's software combines AI with physics-based models to predict how candidate molecules bind to the target and how tightly (binding affinity), and filters them against BioAge's chemistry criteria before anything is made. Deep Origin says this produced multiple structurally distinct hits in under three months.

Why it matters: The target, the number of hits, their potency and the assay were not disclosed, and the announcement is from the companies themselves, so the result cannot yet be assessed. A hit is also far from a drug. Deep Origin is eligible for milestone payments, but the amounts were not given.

Sources: Deep Origin press release
